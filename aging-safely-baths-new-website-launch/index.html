Aging Safely Baths Launches Rebuilt Website With Faster Load Times and Clearer Accessibility Product Information

The walk-in tub and barrier-free shower supplier has moved to a rebuilt site organized around the questions households actually ask before commissioning accessibility work.

Aging Safely Baths Issuing organization September 24, 2026

At a glance

Aging Safely Baths has launched a rebuilt website at agingsafelybaths.com. The site is organized around the configuration decisions households face before commissioning accessibility work, and loads faster than the platform it replaced.

DENVER, Colorado — September 24, 2026

Aging Safely Baths has launched a rebuilt website at agingsafelybaths.com. The new site is organized around the decisions households face when planning accessible bathing — entry height, seating, structural requirements and installation scope — rather than around product categories.

The company, which has supplied walk-in tubs and barrier-free showers since 2012, says the reorganization came from reviewing what customers actually asked during consultations.

"Almost every call starts the same way," said Zachary Tye Wennstedt, founder of Aging Safely Baths. "Somebody describes their bathroom and asks what will fit. The old site made them figure out which product category they were in before it could help. That's backwards."

What changed

  • Configuration-led navigation. Content is grouped by the access requirement — ambulatory, walker, wheelchair, seated bathing — rather than by product name.
  • Faster loading. The site was rebuilt as static pages, which removes the database and plugin overhead of the previous platform.
  • Clearer structural guidance. Information about subfloor and threshold requirements now appears before pricing, since those constraints determine what is possible.
  • Improved mobile layout. Pages reflow to a single column without horizontal scrolling.

Why the structural question comes first

The company says the most common cause of a stalled accessible bathroom project is discovering a structural constraint after a product has already been chosen.

A true zero-threshold shower requires the pan to sit at or below the surrounding floor. In wood-framed construction that means modifying the subfloor. In slab-on-grade construction it can mean cutting the slab. A walk-in tub, by contrast, frequently fits an existing footprint with far less intervention.

"Find out what your floor will let you do, then pick the product," Wennstedt said. "People do it the other way round and then get a surprise."

Context

According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, roughly one in four adults aged 65 and older reports a fall each year. AARP survey work has consistently found that a large majority of adults over 50 would prefer to remain in their current homes as they age.

Availability

The new site is live at agingsafelybaths.com. Barrier-free shower kits are also available through Showers4Less.com.

Frequently asked

What is the difference between a walk-in tub and a barrier-free shower?

A walk-in tub has a sealed door, built-in seating and controls positioned for seated reach, and often fits an existing plumbing footprint. A barrier-free shower has a zero or near-zero entry threshold suitable for wheelchair or walker access, and usually requires work to the subfloor to achieve a level entry.

How long does an accessible bathroom installation take?

It depends primarily on whether structural work is needed. A walk-in tub fitting an existing footprint is a shorter job than a zero-threshold shower requiring the floor to be recessed. Establishing which category a bathroom falls into is the first step in any quote.

Does Aging Safely Baths install nationwide?

Yes. The company supplies and installs walk-in tubs and barrier-free showers for households across the United States and has operated since 2012.
