Notes on the parts people get wrong

The dateline

City and state in caps, an em dash, then the date. It looks like a formality and it is doing real work — it establishes where and when, which is the first thing anyone assessing the news needs to know.

The hash marks

Three hash marks centered after the body mark the end of the release. Everything below them is reference material rather than the announcement itself. It is a convention from the wire era that survives because it is genuinely useful.

The boilerplate

Two or three sentences. This is where people put the marketing copy they were told to keep out of the body, and it is equally wrong there. Facts: what the organization does, since when, from where.

The mistake almost everyone makes Writing the headline first and the lead paragraph to match it. Write the lead paragraph first — get the facts right in one or two sentences — then write the headline from it. Doing it the other way round produces a headline the release cannot support.
