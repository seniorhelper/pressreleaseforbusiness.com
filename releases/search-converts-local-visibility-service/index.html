Search Converts Adds Listing Consistency Audits to Its Small Business Services

The audit identifies where a business's name, address, hours and service description disagree across the listings that describe it.

Search Converts Issuing organization September 8, 2026

At a glance

Search Converts has added listing consistency audits to its small business services, identifying where a company's name, address, hours and service description disagree across the public listings that describe it.

DENVER, Colorado — September 8, 2026

Search Converts has added listing consistency audits to its services for small businesses. The audit reviews public listings that mention a business and identifies where the recorded name, address, phone number, hours or service description disagrees with what the business currently publishes.

The firm says inconsistencies accumulate through ordinary change rather than neglect.

"Nobody sets out to have four different phone numbers on the internet," said Zachary Tye Wennstedt of Search Converts. "You change a number once in 2019 and update the two places you remember. The other eleven are still out there being wrong."

What the audit covers

  • Directory and review platform listings
  • Map service entries
  • Industry registry and trade body records
  • Auto-generated listings the business never created
  • Mismatches between the business's own site and its listings

Why it matters more now

The firm notes that inconsistency has always been a minor local search problem and has become a larger one as AI assistants take on more commercial research.

A ranked list can tolerate ambiguity — a searcher clicks through and works it out. An answer cannot. A system assembling one either resolves which business is being described or names a different one.

Availability

Audits are available to businesses across the United States at searchconverts.com or by telephone at 1-800-481-8638.

Frequently asked

What is a listing consistency audit?

A review of every public listing that mentions a business — directories, review platforms, map services, industry registries — checking whether the name, address, phone number, hours and service description match what the business currently publishes itself.

Why do inconsistent listings matter?

Search and retrieval systems corroborate business information across sources. Where sources disagree, confidence drops, and a system with lower confidence is more likely to surface a competitor it is surer about.

How do listings become inconsistent in the first place?

Usually through ordinary change. A business moves, changes a phone number, drops a service or adjusts hours, updates its own site, and never revisits the dozens of listings created years earlier. Some listings are also auto-generated from stale data the business never entered.
