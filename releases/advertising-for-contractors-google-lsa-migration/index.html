Advertising For Contractors Warns Trades Businesses to Prepare Now as Google Folds Local Services Ads Into Google Ads

Google began migrating US advertisers in August 2026, with remaining accounts moving in phases through 2027. The standalone Local Services dashboard goes away, and manual cost-per-lead controls go with it.

Advertising For Contractors Issuing organization September 25, 2026

At a glance

Advertising For Contractors is advising trades businesses to prepare for Google's migration of Local Services Ads into Google Ads as pay-per-lead Performance Max campaigns, which began for US advertisers in August 2026 and removes the standalone Local Services dashboard and manual cost-per-lead controls.

DENVER, Colorado — September 25, 2026

Advertising For Contractors, a marketing firm serving trades businesses since 2012, is advising contractor clients to prepare for structural changes to Google Local Services Ads, which Google began migrating into the main Google Ads platform for US advertisers in August 2026 with remaining accounts moving in phases through 2027.

The migration moves Local Services campaigns into Google Ads as pay-per-lead Performance Max campaigns. Reporting on the change indicates the campaigns retain the same reach as Local Services Ads, but the separate Local Services dashboard will no longer be available after migration, and lead management moves into the Google Ads interface.

What changes for a contractor

The dashboard goes away

Contractors accustomed to managing leads in the standalone Local Services interface will manage them in Google Ads instead. For an owner who checks leads on a phone between jobs, this is a workflow change rather than a strategic one — but it is a change that arrives without warning if nobody has told them it is coming.

Manual cost-per-lead control is reduced

The more consequential shift is to bidding. Manual maximum cost-per-lead controls, which agencies have used to influence what Google is willing to pay for different lead types, are substantially replaced by automated bidding.

Industry commentary on the change notes this cuts both ways. Accounts that have been constrained by conservative manual bids may see improved volume. Accounts that were using manual controls deliberately — to hold down cost on low-value lead types — lose a lever.

Target CPA behavior across multiple services

Contractors advertising several trades within one campaign are the group most likely to feel the change. A business running both plumbing and HVAC leads has historically used different targets for each, and the new structure handles that differently.

If you get ten leads a month, you will probably not notice. If you get three hundred calls a month, a two-week dip during the transition is real money. Watch the trend, not the headline. Zachary Tye Wennstedt, Advertising For Contractors

Why Local Services still matters

The firm notes that despite the disruption, the channel remains among the strongest-performing for home services. Published industry figures indicate Local Services Ads capture roughly 13.8% of search result clicks where they appear, and that 29% of searchers prefer clicking a Local Services result compared with 11% for conventional Google Ads. Costs commonly run between $25 and $150 per lead depending on trade and market.

The pay-per-lead model means a contractor is charged when a customer actually makes contact rather than for clicks that go nowhere — which is why the firm advises clients to prepare for the migration rather than to abandon the channel.

What to do before your account migrates

  • Confirm your Google Business Profile and Local Services profile match exactly — linking has been mandatory since November 2024 and mismatches cause problems
  • Record your current cost per lead by service type, so you have a baseline to compare against after migration
  • Tighten service area before migration rather than after — an over-broad area generates leads you cannot profitably serve, and you pay for them either way
  • Keep review velocity up; reviews remain a primary ranking input
  • Fix response time first — it is the single largest controllable factor in lead conversion and it does not change with the platform
  • If you run several trades in one campaign, plan for how targets will be handled

The wider context

The firm places the migration alongside a broader shift in how customers find trades businesses, as AI-assisted search results increasingly answer questions directly and recommend providers rather than returning a list of links.

"The platform mechanics change every year and contractors are tired of hearing about it," Wennstedt said. "What does not change is that the business with real reviews, a profile that matches everywhere, clear information about what it does, and somebody who actually answers the phone is the one that wins. That was true before this migration and it will be true after it."
