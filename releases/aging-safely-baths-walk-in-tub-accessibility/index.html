Aging Safely Baths Highlights Bathroom Accessibility Planning as Households Reconsider Aging-in-Place Renovations

The nationwide walk-in tub and barrier-free shower supplier, operating since 2012, is publishing guidance aimed at households making accessibility decisions before a fall rather than after one.

Aging Safely Baths Issuing organization September 25, 2026

DENVER, Colorado — September 25, 2026

Aging Safely Baths, a nationwide supplier and installer of walk-in tubs and barrier-free showers operating since 2012, has published planning guidance for households weighing bathroom accessibility renovations. The guidance addresses a pattern the company says it encounters repeatedly: accessibility work is most often commissioned in response to an injury rather than in anticipation of changing need.

The bathroom is among the most common locations for falls in the home, and the fixtures involved — a high tub wall, a slick threshold, a surface with nothing to hold — are the ones least likely to be reconsidered until something happens.

"Almost nobody calls us because they sat down and planned ahead," said Zachary Tye Wennstedt, founder of Aging Safely Baths. "They call because somebody fell, and now there's a hospital discharge date and a bathroom that isn't going to work. The renovation is the same renovation either way. Doing it on a deadline just makes every decision worse."

The planning window

The company's guidance centers on a straightforward argument: accessibility work commissioned ahead of need is better work, and it costs less in practical terms even where the price is the same.

Households planning in advance can evaluate multiple configurations, compare installers, schedule around their own calendar, and make decisions about finish and fixture that they will live with for years. Households planning under pressure take what is available.

Advance planning also permits a more complete scope. A bathroom reconfigured properly for accessibility addresses entry threshold, seating, grab bar placement, controls reachable from a seated position, lighting and floor surface as a connected set of decisions. Work done urgently frequently addresses only the fixture that failed.

The renovation is the same renovation whether you plan it or react to it. The difference is whether you got to make the decisions. Zachary Tye Wennstedt, Founder, Aging Safely Baths

Product range

Aging Safely Baths is an authorized Bestbath dealer and supplies the full Bestbath product line. The range covers barrier-free and curbed showers, shower pans, Crestline wall systems, tub-shower combinations, walk-in tubs and accessories.

Configurations available through the company include:

  • Walk-in tubs with a sealed door, built-in seating and controls positioned for seated reach, including hydrotherapy configurations
  • Barrier-free showers with a zero or near-zero entry threshold, suitable for wheelchair and walker access
  • Curbed showers where a low threshold is acceptable and the existing plumbing footprint favors it
  • Tub-shower combinations for households that need both and have a single bathroom
  • Wall systems and pans engineered to fit the above without a full structural rebuild

Questions worth answering before commissioning work

  • Who is the bathroom being adapted for, and what is likely to change over the next decade?
  • Is a seated bathing configuration needed, or is a walk-in shower sufficient?
  • Does the entry need to accommodate a wheelchair or walker, now or later?
  • Can the controls be reached from a seated position without stretching?
  • Are grab bars anchored into structure, or into drywall?
  • Is the floor surface rated for wet slip resistance?
  • Does the existing plumbing footprint constrain the options, and at what cost to change it?

Installation and service area

Aging Safely Baths supplies and installs nationwide. The company notes that installation requirements differ substantially by configuration: a barrier-free shower generally requires work to the subfloor to achieve a level entry, while a walk-in tub often fits an existing footprint with less structural intervention. That distinction can materially affect both the cost and the duration of a project, and the company advises households to establish which category their bathroom falls into early.

Availability

Product information, configuration guidance and consultation requests are available at agingsafelybaths.com. Barrier-free shower kits are also available through Showers4Less.com.

Note on this release This announcement describes products and planning considerations. It is not medical advice. Decisions about mobility equipment and home modification for a specific person should involve an occupational therapist or physician familiar with that person's circumstances.
